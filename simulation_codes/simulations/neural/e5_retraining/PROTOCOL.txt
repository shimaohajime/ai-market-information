Finite neural retraining paths

The market has eight public coordinates with balanced target loadings and noisy
cubic releases. The fundamental follows theta = sqrt(0.8) theta_old +
sqrt(0.2) eta, and the payout is D = 1.15 theta + epsilon with SD(epsilon)=0.43.
Private-noise variances are (0.25, 0.09), investor masses are (0.55, 0.45),
risk aversions are (0.9, 1.2), and supply-noise SD is 0.9.

Adoption x is the high-capacity fraction within group 1. Group 1 uses low- and
high-capacity GRUs with widths 4 and 32, identical 16-period information
windows, and constant-affine Gaussian heads. Group 2 uses its private clue and
the current price but no public encoder; its affine coefficients and residual
variance are refitted under each new market law. The zero-mass capacity cell is
also fitted as a counterfactual candidate.

For each x in {0, 0.5, 1}, the calculation starts from a common inherited
trained profile, simulates a frozen-policy market, and follows six simultaneous
retraining updates. The resulting finite path is not asserted to be an all-low
retraining fixed point or an equilibrium comparative static.

For each update:
  1. Freeze all deployed encoder weights, heads, normalizations, and the
     group-2 policy.
  2. Simulate the competitive market recursively. Encoders observe public
     releases, lagged endogenous price, and matured payout; the current clearing
     price enters the next date only. Hidden states reset at every 16-observation
     training window.
  3. Generate training, validation, calibration, and evaluation banks before
     fitting any model. Each path discards 512 burn-in dates. The training bank
     has 512 paths by 2,048 usable observations (1,048,576 observations);
     validation and head-calibration banks each have 65,536 observations, and
     evaluation has 131,072 observations. Windows do not cross paths.
  4. Warm-start both GRUs and refit their encoders and full heads on the common
     training bank. Use eight disjoint chunks, Adam learning rate 0.001 for
     five chunks and decay to 0.00001 over three, batch size 256, weight penalty
     1e-5, gradient-norm clip 3, no early stopping, and reset optimizer state.
  5. Refit group 2 by OLS on all training observations and deploy every newly
     fitted policy simultaneously after all fitting is complete.
  6. Simulate the next frozen-policy market and repeat.

Training improvement is measured on held-out data from the current law, while
deployed accuracy is measured on the next law; these objects are reported
separately. Primitive streams are indexed by seed, update, and bank. Adoption
shares use matched primitives but recursively recomputed prices. Evaluation
streams never enter optimization or model selection, and training data are
fresh across updates.

Report beta, v, iota, investor contributions to A, J=A^2/0.9^2, price moments,
held-out MSE, log score, variance calibration, and incremental private-signal
prediction value from separate full/no-private OLS heads. J is a structural
fresh-price precision measure, not mutual information or total price
informativeness. Coupled initial-price and retained-path-moment diagnostics are
descriptive checks, not proof of global stability, uniqueness, or a population
retraining fixed point. Unit tests check clearing, analytically solvable
constant-policy markets, causal timing, path boundaries, inactive capacity, and
frozen model fingerprints.

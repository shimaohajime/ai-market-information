Slow-fast retraining implementation, 28 October 2026

Economic baseline: the confirmed capacity/private-importance V2 experiment.
The eight public coordinates have balanced target loadings and noisy cubic
releases. theta = sqrt(.8)*theta_old + sqrt(.2)*eta, with unchanged total
fundamental variance. D = 1.15*theta + epsilon, SD(epsilon)=.43. Private noise
variances are (.25,.09), masses (.55,.45), risk aversions (.9,1.2), supply SD .9.

Adoption x is the high-capacity fraction of group 1, not of the whole market.
Group 1 has low/high GRUs of widths 4/32, identical 16-period information
windows and Model-A constant affine heads. Group 2 retains its no-public-
encoder information class, using its private clue and current price; its
affine coefficients and residual variance are refitted on every new law.
All types therefore update within their own unchanged feasible classes.
The zero-mass low or high cell is still fitted as a counterfactual candidate.

Initial weights are the first three completed V2 confirmation seed pairs.
No new independent confirmation claim is made for this implementation study.
At each adoption x in {0,.5,1}, start from the SAME inherited trained profile.
First simulate the frozen-policy market Q_0^F(x). This is not asserted to be
an all-low retraining fixed point, nor an adoption shock from such a fixed point.
Then perform six simultaneous slow updates, retaining all seven deployed states.

For slow epoch n:
  1. Freeze encoder weights, final heads, normalizations and group-2 policy.
  2. Run the competitive market recursively: encoders observe public releases,
     lagged endogenous price and matured payoff; solve the CURRENT clearing
     price; feed it only to the NEXT date. Restart each GRU's hidden state at
     zero for every 16-observation window, exactly as in the training class.
  3. Generate ALL train/validation/calibration/evaluation data before learning.
     Each path discards 512 burn-in periods. The training bank consists of
     512 independent paths with 2048 usable observations each: 1,048,576.
     Validation and independent head-calibration banks each have 65,536
     observations; evaluation has 131,072. No windows cross path boundaries.
  4. Warm-start both GRUs and update encoder and full head on the SAME bank.
     Split 512 paths into eight disjoint chunks; use one complete pass over
     each chunk (one pass through the full bank), Adam .001 for five chunks, then
     decay to .00001 over three. Batch 256, weight penalty 1e-5, clip norm 3.
     No early stopping, no deployed OLS replacement for the neural models.
     Freeze inherited normalization to preserve physical predictions at a
     warm start. Reset optimizer state at each slow update.
  5. Refit group 2 by OLS using all training observations. Deploy ALL newly
     fitted policies together only after every fit has finished.
  6. Generate the next frozen-policy market, then repeat.

This is a finite-sample, finite-optimization approximation to the population
best-response map in S3. Six updates do not by themselves establish equilibrium.
Training improvement is scored on held-out data from Q_n; deployed accuracy
at n+1 is separately scored on Q_{n+1}. These must not be conflated.

Randomness: seed, slow epoch and bank identify primitive shock streams.
Adoption rates share primitives, but prices are always recomputed recursively.
Evaluation primitives are common across slow epochs to sharpen comparisons;
they are never optimizer inputs or model-selection data. Training data are
fresh between slow epochs. Initial-condition coupling checks use other banks.

Diagnostics: beta, v, iota, all active types' contributions to A, J=A^2/.9^2,
price moments, held-out MSE/log score/variance calibration, and private-signal
incremental prediction value from independent full/no-private OLS heads.
J is constant within a frozen-policy fast market under Model A. It changes
across slow updates only when fitted policies change. Do not label J mutual
information or total price informativeness. No S5 upgrade-utility claim here.

Fast diagnostics compare +/-10 initial prices under identical future shocks
after burn-in, and compare price moments across the retained path halves.
Slow diagnostics inspect successive J, forecast and policy changes. A 0.5%
relative tolerance for three successive updates is a descriptive criterion,
not proof of global optimality, uniqueness, or a population retraining fixed point.

Implementation tests independently verify clearing, recovery of an analytically
solvable constant-policy market, finite-window inference, no future-payoff leakage,
inactive-capacity isolation, path boundaries, and frozen model fingerprints.

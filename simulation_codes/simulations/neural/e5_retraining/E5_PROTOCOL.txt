E5 continuation and baseline establishment - 29 October 2026

Source: AI_Trading_Slow_Fast_Retraining_v1_20261028.zip, Library version 2.
The original archive and original results are retained under e5_inputs. All
accepted manuscript files are untouched. Existing economic primitives,
architectures, optimization budgets, data-bank sizes and normalization remain
unchanged. Resume the nine trajectories from their saved round-6 deployments.

Primary extension: twelve simultaneous slow updates, rather than six, for all
three seeds and x in {0, .5, 1}. This first extension is a fixed budget, not a
stopping time selected for a favorable sign. If a longer continuation is run,
record its scope and reason. A completed terminal stage must be reopened to
generate training data; its deployed checkpoint must not change.

The fast-law simulation, burn-in, held-out scoring and model training follow
PROTOCOL.txt. In particular, price generation ends before any refit begins.
The zero-adoption trajectory is the candidate baseline for later adoption
shocks. The other two original trajectories diagnose continuation; their
different early histories do not constitute shocks from the mature baseline.

Original strict settling diagnostic: all successive relative changes in J,
each candidate's MSE, beta and v below .5% for the last three updates. Retain
and report this diagnostic, even if it fails. It is not a population theorem.

Fixed-law audit: at the designated candidate endpoint, independently repeat
one complete refit four times for EACH zero-adoption seed. Every repetition
starts from the identical saved low/high/other profile. Training-bank shocks
and optimizer permutations differ. Bank sizes and update schedules are the
same as in the primary iteration. Generate all data before fitting; do not
deploy audit fits into the data-generating market. Evaluate them using new
held-out shocks, shared across repetitions of each seed. Report changes in J,
beta, v, iota, forecast MSE and forecast/holding RMS. The implied updated J is
computed from fitted coefficients; price moments from the audit evaluation
are still moments of the original frozen law, not of a newly deployed market.

Interpretation: within-seed variation estimates training-data/optimization
noise; the mean one-step update also contains systematic learning drift.
Neither a small sample mean nor a failure to reject zero proves a fixed point.
Report replicate values, within-seed SD, seed-specific mean and t intervals.
For aggregate intervals, average within each of the three initial seeds first;
use those three means as independent units (df=2). No row-level pseudo-replication.

Operational-baseline diagnostic, in addition to the original strict one:
compare means over rounds 7-9 and 10-12, for J and the ACTIVE low/other iotas.
A 1% practical band is used for these market-mechanism quantities. Report the
band explicitly; it does not replace the original .5% successive-update test.
A provisional baseline is supported only if these block drifts are below 1%
for each seed, all seed-specific mean same-law updates are below 1%, and the
aggregate 95% t intervals for the mean same-law updates lie inside +/-1%.
Do not select a seed, average trained policies, calibrate away a failed test,
or change learning rates in order to force this diagnostic to pass. If it
fails, retain the endpoint as a reproducible candidate and state the failure.
Extra same-law optimization, if performed, is reported separately and cannot
silently replace the endpoint.

Baseline artifacts: complete deployed low/high checkpoints, group-2 head,
normalization, economic configuration, seed, slow-round number, fingerprints,
diagnostic status, and regeneration instructions. The counterfactual high
model exists at x=0 but does not influence its prices. Its latest fitting law
is the previous slow epoch, as for all deployed models; no global optimality
or exact same-law best response is presumed at a finite endpoint.

Optional first-auction/frozen-policy diagnostic from the candidate baseline:
use identical baseline pre-auction histories and private/supply primitives,
alter only x, and recompute clearing. Then simulate new fixed-policy laws at
the new x. Keep low, high and other parameters unchanged in both comparisons.
This is distinct from the original high-adoption continuation paths. Under
Model A, J must match exactly between first-auction and frozen-law stages;
price moments and forecasting performance can differ. Do not report these
newly shocked branches as retrained outcomes until actually refitted.

Run commands (Python with numpy, scipy, pandas and torch 2.5.1 available):
  python run.py --workers 5
  python analyze.py
  python audit_baseline.py --round 12 --reps 4 --workers 3
  python summarize_e5.py

Primary experiment uncertainty remains based on three inherited initial
training seeds, not a new independent confirmation cohort. Gaussian primitive
shocks do not guarantee Gaussian endogenous market laws. J is structural
fresh-signal likelihood precision, not mutual information or aggregate welfare.

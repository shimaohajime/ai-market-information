Neural learning dynamics and payout-law robustness

Learning-dynamics calculation:
  Continue the nine finite neural-market profiles from update 6 through update
  12 with unchanged learning budgets and fresh update-indexed data. At adoption
  0.5, also repeat six updates using common primitive innovations and optimizer
  order. Prices and histories are recomputed after every policy update. Four
  independent one-step refits from each fresh update-12 profile measure
  conditional update noise and mean drift; they are not independent markets.

Track J, A, the market-clearing slope, and the low-, high-, and other-group
iota values. J is A^2 divided by supply variance. Descriptive stability screens
require maximum relative changes in J and all three iotas below 0.5% during the
final three updates, and late versus preceding three-state J means within 1%.
A fixed-law drift check places each mean percent change and its t interval in
[-1%, 1%]. A passing screen is not an equilibrium result and a failing screen
does not prove economic instability. A period-two diagnostic records sign
reversals, lag-two versus lag-one distances in [J, iota_low, iota_high,
iota_other], and the continuous values underlying the screen. The short path
cannot rule out longer cycles or establish population convergence.

Payout-law robustness calculation:
  Compare a matched control with two alternative payout processes, holding all
other primitives fixed. Private signals remain theta plus noise. In the
nonaligned specification, 20% of fundamental dividend variance arises from an
orthogonal factor absent from the private signal while unconditional variance
matches the control. In the heteroskedastic specification, payout noise variance
is 0.43^2[1+0.6 tanh(public0/SD(public0))], so the multiplier lies in (0.4,1.6)
and has unconditional mean one. The forecasting head remains constant-affine;
the heteroskedastic setting therefore intentionally uses a misspecified constant
forecast variance. J continues to measure fresh information about theta, not
about the full dividend.

For each payout law, refit low, high, and group-2 policies on a common inherited
law, compare adoption shares 0, 0.5, and 1, then simulate the associated
frozen-policy history adjustment. On every resulting law, refit negligible
low- and high-capacity candidates from the common starting profile and measure
the price-usefulness and computational-upgrade outcomes. No additional
market-participant retraining is performed in this calculation.

Each model or refit uses 512 paths by 2,048 windows and eight training chunks
(4,096 Adam updates); validation uses 64 paths by 1,024 dates, independent
head calibration uses 128 paths by 2,048 dates, and evaluation uses four banks
of 256 paths by 1,024 dates after a 512-date burn-in with a 16-date context.
Primitive streams are matched across adoption shares and payout laws. Public
observer heads are fitted with and without the current price; primary upgrade
values use prediction-trained neural heads and secondary checks use affine
low, high, and joint-encoder heads. Current Gaussian payoff noise is integrated
using its true mean and variance without clipping or a Gaussian-posterior
shortcut. Latent variables are available only for scoring. All values are gross
of computation costs.

Report descriptive uncertainty across the three initial market seeds, conditional
evaluation uncertainty, and exponential-weight concentration. A functional
diagnostic evaluates policies from updates 6--12 on one immutable update-6
market-law anchor per seed and adoption share, tracking normalized RMS forecast
and order changes. It guards against treating stability of J or iota as equality
of complete policies. None of these calculations certify a population learning
equilibrium or a universal information or welfare theorem.

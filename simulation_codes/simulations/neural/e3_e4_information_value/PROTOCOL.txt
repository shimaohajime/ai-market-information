Price usefulness and computational-upgrade value in fixed neural markets

The calculation uses nine fixed finite-learning market profiles: three initial
seeds and three adoption shares (0, 0.5, and 1). The profiles are not treated
as retraining equilibria. Negligible price-taking candidates never enter market
clearing. At every adoption share, the low-width (4) and high-width (32) GRU
candidates share the same seed-specific initial profile and are refitted
independently from market participants.

Both candidates use inherited normalization, a 16-date context, one pass over
1,048,576 training windows, 4,096 Adam updates, and a Gaussian-NLL prediction
objective. The design does not optimize utility or select models on evaluation
performance. Validation uses 64 independent paths of 1,024 observations;
separate affine-head calibration uses 128 paths of 2,048 observations. Four
independent evaluation banks use 256 paths of 1,024 observations after a
512-date burn-in. Training, validation, calibration, and evaluation use
separate bank identifiers. Primitive draws are paired across adoption shares
and candidates, while market prices are recomputed in every market.

Price-usefulness measure:
  Fit affine dividend heads with and without the current price to the low and
  high public encoders. Neither head observes the candidate's private signal.
  Report the held-out absolute MSE reduction from price for the low observer
  (S), the high observer (D), and their difference (T=S-D), along with the
  price-free and price-inclusive forecast gaps. Relative MSE reductions are
  secondary and negative gains are retained. A second specification uses the
  joint public vector (z_low,z_high); it requires both encoders and is reported
  as an implemented-information check, not as a stand-alone high-capacity model
  or an unrestricted conditional expectation.

Computational-upgrade value:
  The primary comparison uses each prediction-trained neural mean and constant
  variance with the same current price and the same private signal:
      h = (mu - R_f p) / (gamma v).
  The certainty equivalent is
      CE = -log E[exp(-gamma h(D-R_f p))] / gamma,
  with zero initial wealth. The current Gaussian payoff innovation has standard
  deviation 0.43 and is integrated analytically. Latent theta is available only
  for scoring, never as candidate or observer information. Evaluation uses
  log-sum-exp without clipping, winsorization, or a Gaussian-posterior shortcut.
  The primary upgrade value is CE_high - CE_low. A secondary affine-head
  recalibration uses an independent calibration bank and reports low, high, and
  joint-encoder specifications separately. All values are gross of computation
  costs.

Uncertainty and validation:
  Report paired seed contrasts and descriptive Student-t intervals with two
  degrees of freedom. A bank-stratified path bootstrap quantifies evaluation
  uncertainty conditional on fitted models; it does not create additional
  training replications. Report certainty-equivalent weight concentration,
  independent-bank and half-sample checks, market fingerprints, and the
  separation of calibration from evaluation. The Gaussian benchmark and the
  direct-versus-integrated utility calculation validate the measurement code.
  These finite diagnostics do not establish retraining equilibrium, nonlinear
  mutual information, aggregate welfare, or net value after computing costs.

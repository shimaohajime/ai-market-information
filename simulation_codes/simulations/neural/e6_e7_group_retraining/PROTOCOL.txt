Group retraining and forecast-component decomposition

The design compares three inherited initial seeds, adoption shares 0, 0.5, and
1, and four update arms: no group refits, group 1 only, group 2 only, and both
groups. Adoption is the high-capacity share within group 1, whose population
mass is 0.55. Private-signal precisions, preferences, and economic primitives
are held fixed. Group 1 refits both GRUs, including the inactive counterfactual
candidate at zero or full adoption. Group 2 refits its affine mean and variance
using its private clue and the current price.

Every arm starts from identical parameters within a seed and adoption share.
The initial comparison is a frozen-policy market after the change in adoption,
not a certified retraining equilibrium. In each updating arm, all training data
are generated before fitting; the disabled group's parameters and normalization
remain fixed, although its forecasts can respond to changing public histories.
The no-refit arm is simulated once and its unchanged law is reused across
horizons.

Each update uses 512 independent paths by 2,048 dates (1,048,576 training
observations), a 512-date burn-in, a 16-date context, 64 validation paths by
1,024 dates, and 128 held-out evaluation paths by 1,024 dates. The neural
specification uses widths 4 and 32, constant-affine Gaussian heads, inherited
normalization, and a one-pass eight-chunk Adam schedule. Training uses the
prediction loss, not trading profits or a separately optimized mechanism
statistic. Primitive and optimizer streams are keyed by seed and update but
not by arm or adoption; prices and histories are nevertheless recomputed for
every arm. Evaluation streams are separate from all training streams.

Retraining estimands for an outcome Y (including J, A, and group
contributions) are:
  R_arm(x,n) = Y_arm(x,n) - Y_none(x,0),
  T_arm(x,n) = R_arm(x,n) - R_arm(0,n),
  I(x,n) = Y_both - Y_group1 - Y_group2 + Y_none.
The x=0 comparator has the same arm, horizon, and initial seed. The total
effect is Y_arm(x,n) - Y_arm(0,n). Percentage decompositions use the single
within-seed denominator Y_none(0,0). Report seed-level contrasts, paired means,
and descriptive Student-t intervals with two degrees of freedom; dates and
correlated arms are not independent initial seeds.

The forecast-component calculation holds the same inherited public history,
fundamental, and supply realization fixed while clearing four post-fit profiles:
old mean/old variance, new mean/old variance, old mean/new variance, and new
mean/new variance. "Mean" includes the encoder, intercept, private-signal
slope, and price slope; "variance" is the fitted constant residual variance.
These are post-fit substitutions, not separately optimized predictors or
retrained equilibria. The symmetric decomposition is:
  Mean = 0.5[(Y10-Y00)+(Y11-Y01)],
  Risk = 0.5[(Y01-Y00)+(Y11-Y10)].
The two components sum exactly to Y11-Y00. For J, the mean channel changes beta
and variance changes the risk-adjusted demand response beta/(gamma v).

For J, A, and iota, per-update contributions telescope under the stated
constant-affine specification. The corresponding x=0 contribution is subtracted
to obtain the adoption-specific cumulative effect. Price-variance swaps are
one-auction diagnostics on the inherited law and are not summed into a
stationary-market identity.

Validation checks identical initial profiles across arms, exact freezing of
disabled groups, generation before learning, continuity between fitted and
deployed policies, positive clearing slopes, clearing residuals, initial-price
coupling, the factorial identities, and the forecast-component identities. The
finite calculations do not establish mutual information, welfare, global
optimality, or a retraining equilibrium.

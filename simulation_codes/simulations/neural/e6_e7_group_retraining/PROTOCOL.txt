E6 group-specific retraining with E7 accounting - 29 October 2026

Fixed starting profiles: three all-low (x=0) update-12 checkpoints from E5.
They did not pass the E5 convergence criteria. All claims below are comparisons
at fixed finite horizons from these candidate baselines, not equilibrium
comparative statics. The high model is an inactive candidate fitted under the
previous slow law, just as the last fit of the active low model precedes its
current deployed law. No fresh baseline refitting is performed here.

Design: 3 inherited initial seeds x 3 adoption shares (0,.5,1) x 4 arms.
Shares refer to high-model adoption within group 1, which has population mass
.55. Both private-signal precisions, risk preferences and all primitives are
held fixed. The four arms are none, group1, group2 and both. Group1 updates
both GRUs, including the counterfactual inactive model at x=0 or x=1. Group2
uses only private signal and price and refits its affine head and variance.

At each adoption share, all four arms start with identical model parameters.
The initial market Q0(x) is the frozen-policy fast law after changing adoption
from the common all-low baseline. Thus stage 0 is after price-history adjustment.
A separate E7 initial-adoption calculation uses inherited all-low histories.
In Model A, J is identical at initial impact and after frozen-policy adjustment.

Run SIX simultaneous slow updates in the three updating arms, with all seven
states saved. At each update, generate the entire training bank first, then
fit only the permitted groups. The other group's parameters and normalization
remain exactly unchanged, although forecasts can respond to changing histories.
All permitted updates are deployed together. Each arm generates its own
endogenous prices and training histories. The none arm simulates its unchanged
fast law once; its exact fixed profile and common-evaluation metrics are reused
at every horizon. This is not counted as seven independently simulated states.

Training observations: 512 independent paths x 2048 dates = 1,048,576.
Burn-in: 512; context: 16 dates, no windows across paths. Neural validation:
64 x 1024; held-out evaluation: 128 x 1024. The separate E5 head-calibration
bank is not needed here, because E6/E7 concern deployed coefficients rather
than private-signal incremental prediction value. Model A, widths 4 and 32,
constant affine heads; inherited normalization; Adam and the E5 one-pass,
eight-chunk schedule unchanged. Learning uses the S2 prediction loss, not
trading profits or an optimized mechanism statistic.

Random streams: training uses seed, 60000+slow_round, bank 1; validation uses
bank 50. Evaluation uses seed, 70000, bank 60 at all horizons. Arm and adoption
share are intentionally excluded from primitive and optimizer random keys.
Prices are always recursively recomputed for each arm. This provides common
random numbers without copying prices across different economic laws. Test
streams are separate from every training stream. There is no sign-based
selection, seed dropping, or stopping rule based on the observed effect.

E6 estimands, for Y = J (also A and group contributions):
  R_arm(x,n) = Y_arm(x,n) - Y_none(x,0): raw retraining response.
  T_arm(x,n) = R_arm(x,n) - R_arm(0,n): adoption-specific extra retraining.
The x=0 comparator uses the SAME update arm, horizon and initial seed. It
controls for ordinary residual adaptation of the imperfect baseline.
  I(x,n) = Y_both - Y_group1 - Y_group2 + Y_none.
  T_both = T_group1 + T_group2 + [I(x,n)-I(0,n)].
Also report the direct frozen/impact adoption effect and the total effect
Y_arm(x,n)-Y_arm(0,n). All additive percentage decompositions use the single
fixed denominator Y_none(0,0) within each seed. Report seed-level values,
paired means and t intervals over the three seed-level contrasts (df=2).
Do not treat dates or the correlated arms as independent initial seeds.

E7 mechanism diagnostic: on exactly the same inherited history, theta and
supply realizations, clear four post-fit profiles: old mean/old variance,
new mean/old variance, old mean/new variance, new mean/new variance. Mean
means the whole fitted forecast mean, including encoder, intercept, private
slope and price slope. Variance means only the constant fitted v. These are
post-fit swaps, NOT separately optimized predictors or retrained equilibria.
Record both mixed profiles and the interaction. Allocate the interaction
symmetrically via the two-order (Shapley) decomposition:
  Mean = .5[(Y10-Y00)+(Y11-Y01)]
  Risk = .5[(Y01-Y00)+(Y11-Y10)].
These sum exactly to Y11-Y00. For J, the mean channel works through beta,
while v changes the risk-adjusted demand response beta/(gamma*v).

For structural J, A and each iota, sum the per-update contributions over
updates: this telescopes exactly because those statistics do not depend on
the history distribution under Model A. Subtract the corresponding x=0 sum
to obtain adoption-specific cumulative mean/risk effects. For price variance,
the swap is only a one-auction diagnostic on the old law; its per-update
contributions must NOT be interpreted as the total change in the next law's
price variance or summed into a stationary-market accounting identity.

A separate initial-adoption E7 diagnostic changes the upgrading cell from
low mean/low v to high mean/high v under inherited all-low histories. Its
contributions are kept distinct from cumulative retraining contributions.

Validation: existing six engine tests plus four tests of the new accounting.
Check identical round-0 profiles across all arms, exact freezing of disabled
groups, first-update equivalence of corresponding permitted fits, no change
during data generation, generation before learning, saved-next/deployed-next
continuity, positive clearing slope, clearing residual, initial-price coupling,
all E7 identities and all E6 factorial/control identities.

Interaction diagnostic: J=A^2/supply_variance is nonlinear. For each x,n,
write a0,a1,a2,ab for aggregate A in the four arms. The J interaction is
2(a1-a0)(a2-a0)/supply_variance plus
[ab^2-(a1+a2-a0)^2]/supply_variance. The first term is mechanical; the second
reflects nonadditivity in learned A. Subtract x=0 for each term. First-update
A additivity and a zero second term are checked because the fitted blocks
then use the same starting law. Later J interaction must not automatically
be described as entirely a learning feedback.

# Independently optimized mean and risk ablations

## Research question

What changes when mean-only and variance-only high-capacity forecasters **re-optimize their permitted component separately**, rather than swapping components of a jointly trained forecast model after training?

In the nonlinear, heteroskedastic eight-factor market, both operations can improve Gaussian NLL, but the price's informational performance for a finite-history external decoder can move in either direction. Restricted reoptimization and post-hoc swaps differ materially.

## Design

- Competitive one-period CARA market: asset supply shock, multiple atomistic investor types, private signals, endogenous price clearing.
- A nonaligned payoff with conditional volatility loading multiplied by 0.7 / 1.0 / 1.3. This is the only variation among the three calibration families.
- SSM and GRU, 4-state original and 12-state higher-capacity neural predictors; 32 periods of pre-auction public input. Head B has state-dependent mean/private and price slopes and variance.
- Training seeds 11 / 23 / 37; 2,200-period frozen diagnostic-incumbent market data per training cell, with chronological training and validation splits, max 270 Adam steps and early checkpoint selection.
- Four independently trained arms per cell: 00 = learned low-capacity mean and variance; 10 = high-capacity mean optimized under the fixed low-variance policy; 01 = high-capacity variance optimized with fixed low mean; 11 = high-capacity mean/variance optimized jointly. The component not allowed to change is **literally generated by the same frozen low-capacity model**.
- For each training cell, two independently drawn market-bank pairs of 4,200 periods; all price auctions reuse exactly the same public history and exogenous shocks across arms. External OOS ridge-linear decoder uses public macro and 3 lags plus candidate price.
- 2 architectures × 3 calibrations × 3 training seeds = **18 training cells**, 4 models/cell = **72 checkpoint files**, 2 independent OOS market-bank pairs/cell = **36 paired comparisons / 144 arm-level market outcomes**.

## Run

```bash
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python experiment.py --steps 270
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 python compare_swaps.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python summarize.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

`experiment.py --quick` runs a one-cell smoke test but **overwrites output CSVs**. Run the full command afterward to regenerate the complete results. Inference caches are initialized automatically, and all saved models are read by `compare_swaps.py`.

## Output guide

- `results/independently_optimized_arms.csv`: 144 arm-by-bank observations; model NLL on a common inherited law, decoder gains, risk, microfounded price-clearing diagnostics.
- `results/paired_effects.csv`: 36 same-shock-bank paired contrasts vs 00; **not** 36 statistically independent trained AI systems.
- `results/training_cell_summary.csv`: 18 independent training cells, with the two evaluation banks averaged within each.
- `results/swap_vs_reoptimized_effects.csv`: 36 matched contrasts with saved joint-model post-training swaps as a diagnostic alternative, not an independently optimized treatment.
- `results/training_details.json`: validation curves, stop iteration, performance per fitted restricted learner.
- `models/*.pt`: all 72 independently trained low/restricted/joint neural model checkpoints, complete with parameters, normalizations and fit metadata.
- `figures/*.png`: three plots of decoder effects, NLL gains, and post-hoc swap vs reoptimization.
- `tests/test_restricted.py`: tests on calibration, head masking, population Gaussian identity, market clearing/data integrity and intervention differences.
- `vendor/marketlab`: reproducible baseline competitive market simulator, resource-constrained forecaster, and deployment utilities; `vendor/features.py` contains only causal public-history features.

## Limits and interpretation

1. The inherited data-generating market uses **fixed diagnostic incumbent policies**, not an exact learning equilibrium. The experiment is first-deployment, not long-run retraining.
2. The mean/variance learner is separately trained in each restriction, but the resulting nonlinear neural solution is an approximate empirical optimizer, **not a certified global population optimum**. Optimizers and validation stopping affect results.
3. The decoder is a **finite-history OOS linear estimator** of the target fundamental and is not conditional mutual information, full-information Blackwell informativeness or social welfare.
4. The tested economic calibrations vary only conditional volatility amplitude, not arbitrary macro dynamics or signal technologies; other calibrations may behave differently.
5. Across-economy statistical generalization is limited: three seeds per calibration and three correlated calibrations do not support broad uncertainty claims.
6. The aligned theoretical private-information comparison and the nonaligned decoder-gain calculation answer different questions.

## Empirical synopsis

Per architecture, averaged over nine independently trained calibration/seed cells and two paired evaluation banks/cell:

| Arm vs old low-capacity baseline | SSM decoder Δ (pp) | GRU decoder Δ (pp) | SSM NLL gain | GRU NLL gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Independently optimized mean only | +0.343 | +0.196 | −0.00065 | +0.01231 |
| Independently optimized risk only | −0.181 | −0.047 | +0.00238 | +0.00308 |
| Jointly optimized mean and risk | +0.172 | +0.731 | +0.00130 | +0.01501 |

All prices cleared with maximum absolute residual about 1.33×10⁻¹⁵ and minimum aggregate slope 0.652. A positive decoder delta is **not** a welfare increase or an information revelation theorem.
